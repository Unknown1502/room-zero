# Character bible

Three characters: the courier, the Surveyor and the house. Visual design is in `03-art-and-audio/character-design.md`.

## The Courier

| | |
|---|---|
| Role | Player character |
| Seen as | Two gloved hands and the cuffs of a coat. Nothing else, ever |
| Scale | Human. The house is three times too large for them |
| Age and identity | Adult, unnamed, unspecified. The hands are gloved so that they can be anyone's |
| Voice | None |
| Wants | To deliver the letter |
| Knows | Almost nothing on waking. Their hands remember the brass |
| Trait shown by play | Patient, practical, good with fittings. They do not fight |

**Costume, as far as it is seen.** Oiled leather work gloves, darkened at the fingertips, with a stitched patch on the left palm. A heavy wool coat cuff in faded postal blue-grey with two brass buttons. A canvas satchel strap, seen only as the place the letter returns to.

**The courier's handwriting** is the most important piece of characterisation. It is on the envelope, the card and every envelope in the pigeonholes. It is small, upright, careful and slightly cramped: the hand of someone who has written this address many times. The other envelope, ROOM 1, is in a visibly different hand, looser and slanted.

**Do not** give the courier a face, a reflection, a shadow with a recognisable silhouette, a name, a line of dialogue or a signature garment in a saturated colour.

## The Surveyor

| | |
|---|---|
| Role | Antagonist; in the end, not an enemy |
| Nature | The house's caretaker and keeper of its record |
| Scale | About 7.5 m upright; usually stooped to 5 m |
| Voice | None. It communicates by knocking, by its tools and by the direction of its ring |
| Wants | Every joint measured. Everything where the record says |
| Fears | Nothing. It can be puzzled: a gap where a doorway was makes it stop and measure |
| Trait shown by behaviour | Deliberate, incurious about the player as a person, incapable of haste or malice |

**Tools.** A steel tape on a brass reel worn at the wrist. A plumb line with a turned lead bob. A carpenter's pencil behind where an ear would be. Its body is its notebook: it writes measurements on its own plaster.

**Movement.** It folds and unfolds. It places a hand on a door frame before passing through. It never turns its back on a joint it has not measured. It never looks at the player with anything, because it has no face; it turns the opening of its ring toward what it is going to.

**Relationship to the player.** For most of the chapter it is a threat that follows rules. In the last thirty seconds it is revealed as the thing that has been trying, every night, to put the lodger back, and tonight has somewhere to put them.

**Do not** give it eyes, a mouth, teeth, a growl, a scream, a run, a lunge, clothing, or any feature resembling a human caricature. It is architecture that walks.

## The House

| | |
|---|---|
| Role | Setting and third character |
| Nature | A boarding house whose rooms are separable sections hung in a void |
| Voice | Groans, settles, thuds. A distant letterbox flap. At the end, a long slow folding |
| Wants | To close, each night, with everything in its place |
| Trait shown by behaviour | Heavy, obedient to brass, indifferent |

**Rules it keeps.** Rooms move only on their rails. Doorways mate only like to like. It responds to hands on brass and to nothing else. It closes when the last joint is made.

**How it treats the courier.** It was not built for them and does not notice them, with one exception: the brass grips, the rails, the signal lamps and Room Zero are all at courier scale. Someone fitted this house so that a small person could work it. Who did so is not answered.

## Absent characters

- **The lodger of Room 1.** One envelope in a different hand. Never seen.
- **The residents the house was built for.** Three times human size, long gone. Seen only in the height of door handles and the depth of stair treads.
