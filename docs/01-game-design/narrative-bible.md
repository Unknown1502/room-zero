# Narrative bible

## Premise

A boarding house hangs in a place with no ground. Its rooms are separate and can be parted and joined. Each night the house closes: its rooms fold together like the flaps of an envelope, and whatever is not where the record says it should be is left outside.

One room, the smallest, is taken apart every night. Its number is 0. Its lodger wakes at the front door with a letter in their hand.

## What is actually going on

The courier is the lodger of Room Zero.

Every evening the house unmakes the room, because the record says there is no Room 0: a house's rooms are numbered from 1. The lodger knows this will happen. Before it does, they write themselves a letter, address it to their own room, and leave it where they will wake. The letter is not a message. It is an address, so that someone who has forgotten where they live will carry it there.

The Surveyor keeps the record. It measures every joint in the house so that the record is true, and it returns anything out of place to where it was last recorded. When it reaches the courier it does not harm them. It measures them, finds no entry, and carries them back to the last place they were stamped: the last postmark.

The letter is *unreturned* because, every night before this one, the courier did not arrive before the house closed. The pigeonholes in Room Zero hold the letters from those nights, each with fewer postmarks than a complete journey.

Tonight the courier arrives. The letter is returned to its sender. The card inside says what the address always meant: **You are the return address.** And the Surveyor, at the round door, finally has something to measure. Its incomplete ring, closed by the door's own arc, makes a zero. The record now has a Room 0, with one lodger. The house can close with them inside.

## What the player is told, and when

Nothing is explained in words beyond the following. The reveal must be reconstructible from what is seen.

| Moment | What is shown | What it plants |
|---|---|---|
| Wake | A letter in your hand, in a careful hand: ROOM 0. *Return before the house closes.* | A destination, a deadline, an instruction that reads two ways |
| First seam | One distant knock after you move the stair | Something keeps count of what you do |
| The landing | A small lit lantern far out in the void | The destination exists and is small |
| Each checkpoint | A postmark stamps itself on the envelope | You are being recorded |
| The Surveyor | It measures gaps and joints, not you | It is a keeper, not a hunter |
| Being caught | It frames you in its ring; you wake at the last postmark with a chalk tally on the envelope | It puts things back |
| Room Zero | Everything fits your hands. A coat with your cuff. A warm kettle | This is where you live |
| The pigeonholes | Many envelopes like yours, with fewer postmarks | You have tried before |
| The card | *You are the return address.* | The letter was never for someone else |
| The ring at the door | An incomplete zero made whole | It was looking for this |
| One envelope in another hand: ROOM 1 | | You are not the only lodger |

## Themes

- **Return.** To send something back; to come back; an address to return to.
- **Record and place.** A thing belongs where it is written down. A person without an entry is put back at the door.
- **Labour.** Finding your way home is work done with the hands, every night.
- **Scale.** The house was not built for you. One room was.

## The second reading of the title line

*Return before the house closes.* On waking it reads as a warning from someone else: come back in time. At the end it reads as an instruction the courier wrote to themselves, and as a description of what a returned letter is.

## Tone

Quiet, uneasy, dry. A fairytale told by someone who files things. Dread comes from size, patience and counting. There is no blood, no body horror, no cruelty, no jump scare. The ending is not a twist of the knife. It is strange and oddly gentle, and it leaves a door open.

## What is deliberately left open

- Who built the house, and why it closes.
- Whether the Surveyor understands what it is doing.
- Who lives in Room 1.
- Whether tonight changes tomorrow night.

Nothing in the vertical slice depends on answering these.

## Originality notes

The story contains no child protagonist, no hunger or eating motif, no named location shared with another work, no captor figures drawn from adult caricature, and no escape narrative. Its central image, a lodger who posts themselves home, was written for this project.
