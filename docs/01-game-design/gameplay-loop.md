# Gameplay loop

## Three time scales

### Moment loop: one seam move (5 to 15 seconds)

| Step | Player does | Game does | Feedback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approach | Moves a hand toward a grip | Detects proximity | Grip brightens and "breathes" once |
| Take | Pinches and holds | Starts a manipulation; if latched and unbraced, refuses | Leather creak; latch rattle if refused |
| Move | Moves the hand along the rail | Maps hand position to the seam parameter; clamps at limits and blocks | Room moves with lag; grinding scaled to speed; dust at the seam |
| Read | Watches the doorways | Evaluates what would connect at the nearest detent | Warm line gathers round doorways that will mate; ash outline where a path is blocked |
| Release | Opens the hand | Chooses detent or return; animates the settle | Room seats with a thud, or drifts back with a falling groan |
| Commit | Nothing | Recomputes the graph; evaluates puzzles; informs the Surveyor | Doorway light holds for a second; lamps light or go dark; the count, if it replans |

The player's decision in this loop is where to let go. Everything else is reading.

### Puzzle loop: one sequence (60 to 100 seconds)

1. **Arrive** at a vantage with a station. A postmark stamps the envelope.
2. **Survey** the rooms across the void. The intended reading order is: where am I trying to get to, what shape is its doorway, what do I have that shape.
3. **Try** a move. The first obvious move is designed to be informative, not correct: the half-way rest, the first working door, the blocked arm.
4. **Understand** the one idea of the puzzle from what the house showed.
5. **Solve** in one to three moves.
6. **Traverse** to the next vantage.

If the player is idle at step 2 or 3, the envelope writes a hint (tier 1 at 40 s, tier 2 at 80 s, tier 3 at 120 s).

### Chapter loop (5 to 7 minutes)

Teach (Arrival), practise (Staircase), twist (Wrong Door), pressure (Surveyor), synthesis (Room Zero), release (ending). Each sequence adds one element and removes none.

## The loop under pressure

When the Surveyor is active the same moment loop runs, but the player is now reading two things: the doorways, and the count. The decision gains a second dimension, timing: when to let go, as well as where.

| The Surveyor is | The player's useful moves |
|---|---|
| Two or more doorways away, approaching | Cut a doorway on its route; or make a new joint elsewhere to draw it |
| At a brink, measuring a gap | Nothing needed. It will replan in a few seconds. Use the time |
| Walking to a fresh joint | Wait until it has passed through, then remove the doorway behind it |
| One doorway away | Hop away through an open doorway, or cut that doorway if it has not started to cross |
| With a hand on your door frame | The seam is held. Hop away |

## What the player is never asked to do

- React in under two seconds. The fastest required response in the chapter has a window of about twelve seconds, and it recurs.
- Hold a posture for more than about ten seconds.
- Reach above shoulder height or below the knee.
- Remember a code, a colour or a sequence from an earlier room.
- Read more than a dozen words at once.

## Reward schedule

| Reward | When |
|---|---|
| The seating thud and the ring of light | Every successful connection |
| A new vista | Every hop |
| A postmark | Every sequence |
| Silence after the count | When the Surveyor is cut off |
| A room your own size | Once |
